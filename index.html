About me

I started out in biosystems engineering at Michigan State, spent a year on a manufacturing floor making cleanrooms and production lines run better, then came to Carnegie Mellon for two master's degrees: one in biomedical engineering and one in engineering and technology innovation management.

Somewhere in there I realized the hardest part of medical innovation isn't the engineering. It's getting clinicians, investors, engineers, and regulators to believe in the same thing. That's the part I love.

  • I bring people along Raised $700K+ by building financial models and pitch decks investors bought into, and represented the U.S. at the Greater Zurich Area HealthTech Roadshow.
  • I build bridges with clinicians Designed and ran an IRB-approved clinical study with UPMC, from protocol and endpoints to data collection.
  • I lead from the bench Was the sole product developer through early prototypes, then recruited and managed a four-person engineering team.
  • I make things work better Doubled assembly output and cut coating waste by 25% as a quality and manufacturing engineer.
